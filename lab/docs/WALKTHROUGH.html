WHAT THIS IS REALLY TRAINING

The six meta-skills of a data engineer

ClickHouse is the gym equipment; these are the muscles. Every exercise above trains at least one:

1. Design backwards from queries

Amateurs model the data they have; engineers model the questions that will be asked. Sort keys, rollups, and sync patterns all fall out of a written list of the top query shapes — produced before any CREATE TABLE.

trained by: 01 · 05 · 30

2. Measure, never guess

Every performance claim is testable in under a minute. The discipline is predicting the number before running — a wrong prediction is a located gap in your model, and closing those gaps is the entire mechanism of mastery.

trained by: 10 · 12 · 13 · every torture script

3. Speak in trade-offs

There are no free optimizations, only purchases: storage for read speed, durability for throughput, accuracy for latency, freshness for cost. Seniority is being able to name the price of everything you ship — to machines and to stakeholders.

trained by: 03 · 06 · 13 · 22

4. Treat data as having a lifecycle

Hot, warm, cold, gone — retention is part of the schema, not an afterthought. Systems whose disks fill on schedule instead of by surprise are the ones that stay cheap and stay up.

trained by: 07 · 11 · 30

5. Build for failure on purpose

You reproduced "Too many parts" voluntarily, starved merges, OOM'd a query against a ceiling. Engineers who rehearse failure in the lab recognize it instantly in production — and design the budgets (quotas, caps, dead-letter paths) that make it boring.

trained by: 11 · 14 · 21

6. Integrate through standards, not glue

Parquet, Kafka, SQL interfaces, CDC — the less bespoke code between systems, the less there is to break at 3am. The best pipeline is a FROM clause; the second best is three CREATE statements.

trained by: 20 · 21 · 22 · 31
